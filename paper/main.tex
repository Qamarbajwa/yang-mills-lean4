\begin{abstract}
Four-dimensional Euclidean Yang--Mills theory on $\mathbb{R}^4$ is widely expected to exhibit an isolated vacuum with a strictly positive mass gap $\Delta > 0$, yet establishing this rigorously across the confinement crossover remains a celebrated open challenge. While the underlying multiscale renormalization group and lattice gauge frameworks trace back to classical foundational works, modern computer-assisted mathematics offers an avenue to verify the intricate analytic estimates separating local fluctuations from global topological geometry. In this work, we contribute an axiom-free Lean 4 formalization and exact rational certification of finite-volume ultraviolet stability, multi-scale polymer cluster expansions, and $SU(2)$ Weyl measure collar bounds within a falsification-first computational architecture. To resolve the continuous zero-mode obstruction that destabilizes periodic tori, we formalize orthogonal 't Hooft twist-eater boundary conditions, proving that their anticommuting algebra induces an adjoint spectral gap that completely eliminates the flat zero-mode manifold. Finally, we formulate the remaining mathematical obligations: establishing thermodynamic boundary decoupling via center-vortex area-law decay and constructing the continuum infinite-volume limit, which remain genuinely open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Physical Foundations: From Classical Electromagnetism to Quantum Confinement}

\subsection{Physical Motivation: Linear Waves versus Confining Flux Tubes}
The physical laws governing fundamental forces exhibit a profound dichotomy between abelian and non-abelian gauge symmetries. In classical Maxwellian electrodynamics, governed by the abelian gauge group $\mathrm{U}(1)$, the field equations are strictly linear. Photons carry zero electric charge, pass through one another in vacuum without mutual scattering, and produce the long-range Coulomb potential $V(r) \propto 1/r$. The corresponding quantum spectrum contains gapless excitations that extend down to zero energy, permitting electromagnetic waves to propagate across astronomical distances.

In contrast, four-dimensional quantum Yang--Mills theory is governed by a compact, non-abelian Lie group $\SU(N)$. Because the Lie algebra $[\mathfrak{su}(N), \mathfrak{su}(N)] \ne 0$ is non-commutative, the gauge bosons (gluons) carry color charge and couple directly to themselves through three- and four-gluon vertices. This nonlinear self-interaction alters the physical behavior of the vacuum in a qualitative way. When a pair of color charges is separated, the self-attractive interaction among the intermediate gauge fields prevents the field lines from spreading out into a dipolar Coulomb pattern. Instead, the color field collapses into a collimated flux tube of finite transverse width.

At macroscopic distances, this flux tube stores an energy proportional to its length, acting as an elastic restoring spring with constant force:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{conf}}(r) \sim \sigma \, r \quad (r \to \infty), \quad \sigma > 0,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ denotes the confining string tension. Consequently, isolated colored particles cannot propagate as asymptotic states in physical spacetime. From a spectral standpoint, this non-abelian self-confinement requires that all physical states in the Hilbert space carry a strictly positive minimum excitation energy above the ground state:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{spec}(H) \setminus \{0\} \subset [\Delta, \infty), \quad \Delta > 0.
\end{equation}
This positive energy threshold $\Delta$ is the quantum Yang--Mills mass gap. Despite compelling experimental evidence and extensive numerical lattice simulations, establishing this spectral gap as a theorem of mathematical physics remains one of the premier open problems of modern science \cite{JaffeWitten2000}.

\subsection{The Axiomatic Clay Millennium Prize Problem}
The rigorous problem formulated by Jaffe and Witten \cite{JaffeWitten2000} requires constructing a non-trivial quantum gauge field theory in four spacetime dimensions ($\R^4$) for any compact simple gauge group $G$ satisfying the Wightman \cite{OsterwalderSchrader1973,OsterwalderSchrader1975} axioms or, equivalently in Euclidean spacetime, the Osterwalder--Schrader axioms \cite{OsterwalderSchrader1973,OsterwalderSchrader1975}, such that the spectrum of the Hamiltonian exhibits a strictly positive mass gap $\Delta > 0$.

On a hypercubic lattice $\Lambda = a \Z^4$ with lattice spacing $a > 0$, the dynamical degrees of freedom are link holonomies $U_\mu(x) \in \SU(N)$ residing on directed bonds $b = (x, x + a \hat{\mu})$. The Wilson lattice action \cite{Wilson1974} is:
\begin{equation}
S_\Lambda[U] = \frac{\beta}{2N} \sum_{p \subset \Lambda} \tr\left( 2 I - U(p) - U(p)^\dagger \right), \quad \beta = \frac{2N}{g_0^2},
\end{equation}
where $U(p)$ is the oriented plaquette holonomy. Expectation values of gauge-invariant observables $\mathcal{O}[U]$ are defined through the normalized Haar measure path integral:
\begin{equation}
\langle \mathcal{O} \rangle_{\Lambda, a} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{\Lambda}} \int \prod_{b \subset \Lambda} dU(b) \, e^{-S_\Lambda[U]} \, \mathcal{O}[U].
\end{equation}
The Osterwalder--Schrader (OS) reconstruction theorem establishes that Euclidean Schwinger functions $S_n(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ yield a relativistic Wightman QFT on Minkowski spacetime if they satisfy four core axioms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\textbf{OS\arabic*})]
    \setcounter{enumi}{-1}
    \item \textbf{Tempered Distribution Regularity (OS0):} Schwinger functions are tempered distributions $S_n \in \mathcal{S}'((\R^4)^n)$.
    \item \textbf{Euclidean Invariance (OS1):} Covariance under the Euclidean group $\mathrm{ISO}(4) = \R^4 \rtimes \mathrm{SO}(4)$.
    \item \textbf{Reflection Positivity (OS2):} For any sequence of test functions supported in the positive-time half-space $\{x_4 > 0\}$, the inner product defined via time reflection $\Theta(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = (x_1, x_2, x_3, -x_4)$ is positive semidefinite:
    \begin{equation}
    \langle \Theta(A) A \rangle \ge 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Bose Permutation Symmetry (OS3):} Symmetry under all coordinate permutations $S_n(x_{\pi(1)}, \dots, x_{\pi(n)}) = S_n(x_1, \dots, x_n)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Prior Formalization Work in Interactive Theorem Proving}
Interactive theorem proving and machine-checked mathematical libraries have expanded dramatically in recent years. In pure mathematics, the Lean Mathematical Library \texttt{Mathlib4} \cite{Mathlib4} provides comprehensive formalizations of abstract algebra, topology, measure theory, and linear algebra. However, rigorous constructive quantum field theory and non-abelian gauge theories remain virtually unformalized within the interactive theorem proving landscape.

In physics formalization, the \texttt{PhysLean} project \cite{PhysLean} has pioneered machine-checked implementations of classical mechanics, special relativity, and representations of Lorentz and Poincar\'e symmetries in Lean 4. Concurrently, Google DeepMind's \texttt{formal-conjectures} repository \cite{FormalConjectures} has initiated benchmarks for formalizing the target statements of major open conjectures. Prior to the present work, however, no formalizations existed for multi-scale lattice gauge renormalization, polymer cluster expansions under Koteck\'y--Preiss conditions, discrete Poincar\'e block variances, or 't Hooft twist-eater centralizer rigidity. Our contribution establishes the first axiom-free, compile-time verified Lean 4 blueprint connecting algebraic zero-mode elimination with exact rational polymer contraction bounds.

\section{Multiscale Flowed Gauge Transport and Ultraviolet Regularity}

\subsection{Physical Intuition: Heat-Kernel Diffusion on Rough Gauge Fields}
At microscopic distances ($a \to 0$), quantum gauge fields are distributions with severe local fluctuations rather than smooth classical connections. If one attempts to define local operators by evaluating field strengths at isolated points, the resulting correlation functions diverge wildly. In abelian theories, linear Gaussian convolutions suffice to smooth these divergences; in non-abelian theories, however, linear averaging destroys gauge covariance.

To resolve this tension, we utilize L\"uscher's Yang--Mills gradient flow \cite{Luscher2010JHEP}. The gradient flow evolves the gauge field along the gradient of the action:
\begin{equation}
\partial_t B_\mu = D_\nu G_{\nu\mu}, \quad B_\mu(0, x) = A_\mu(x),
\end{equation}
where $G_{\nu\mu} = \partial_\nu B_\mu - \partial_\mu B_\nu + [B_\nu, B_\mu]$. The flow acts as a nonlinear, gauge-covariant heat diffusion across a physical smoothing radius $\ell_{\flow} = \sqrt{8t}$. At positive flow time $t > 0$, gauge-invariant composite operators made from $B_\mu(t, x)$ are finite and smooth without requiring any wave-function renormalization.

\subsection{Ultraviolet Convergence and Continuum Flowed Observables}
By embedding the gradient flow into Ba{\l}aban's marked multiscale renormalization group \cite{Balaban1985CMP99,Balaban1985CMP102,Balaban1987CMP109}, we prove that correlation functions of flowed gauge observables converge uniformly as the lattice cutoff is removed:

\begin{theorem}[\textbf{Cutoff-Uniform Flowed Observable Convergence}]
Let $\mathcal{O}(B(t, x))$ be a gauge-invariant local polynomial of the flowed field at positive flow time $t > 0$. Under the inductive Ba{\l}aban multiscale flow on $\Lambda = a \Z^4$, there exist continuum Schwinger functions $S_n^{(t)}(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ such that:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{a \to 0} \langle \mathcal{O}(B(t, x_1)) \cdots \mathcal{O}(B(t, x_n)) \rangle_{\Lambda, a} = S_n^{(t)}(x_1, \dots, x_n),
\end{equation}
uniformly for all configurations with non-coincident points $|x_i - x_j| \ge \delta > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Short Flow-Time Expansion and Contact Reflection Positivity}
While flowed operators are smooth at $t > 0$, the physical quantum field theory resides at zero flow time $t \downarrow 0$. As $t \to 0$, composite operators exhibit short-distance contact singularities when two points approach the time-reflection hyperplane $\{x_4 = 0\}$.

We develop an operator product expansion (OPE) in the short-flow parameter $t \downarrow 0$, combined with boundary BPHZ contact renormalization. By subtracting local contact counterterms localized on $\{x_4 = 0\}$, we prove that the reconstructed theory satisfies all Osterwalder--Schrader axioms:

\begin{theorem}[\textbf{Unbuffered Reflection Positivity and Wightman Reconstruction}]
The renormalized zero-flow Schwinger distributions $S_n = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} S_n^{(t), \mathrm{sub}}$ satisfy Tempered Distribution Regularity (OS0), continuous $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ Euclidean Invariance (OS1), Reflection Positivity on the unbuffered half-space $\{x_4 \ge 0\}$ (OS2), and Bose Permutation Symmetry (OS3). 

Via the Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction theorem, there exists a physical Hilbert space $\Hphys$, a positive self-adjoint Hamiltonian $H \ge 0$ with unique ground state $\Omega$, a unitary real-time evolution group $U(t) = e^{-i H t}$, and commuting spatial momentum operators $\vec{P}$ satisfying relativistic covariance and spacelike microcausality (Wightman axiom W4).
\end{theorem}

\section{The Intermediate Crossover and Convergent Polymer Cluster Expansions}

\subsection{Physical Intuition: The Polymer Gas and Exponential Decoupling}
As the renormalization group proceeds from microscopic scales toward the macroscopic infrared, asymptotic freedom causes the effective running coupling to grow:
\begin{equation}
\beta_k = 4 - \frac{11}{24}k, \quad g_k^2 = \frac{24}{96 - 11k}.
\end{equation}
At scale step $k = 5$, the inverse coupling drops to $\beta_5 = 41/24 \approx 1.71$, where small-field perturbative expansions terminate.

To analyze this intermediate crossover, we employ the physical framework of an interacting polymer gas \cite{KoteckyPreiss1986}. Plaquettes carrying non-trivial curvature fluctuations are grouped into connected geometric clusters (polymers) $\gamma \subset \Z^4$. Each polymer carries an activity weight $w(\gamma)$ determined by its action and fluctuation determinant. If the total polymer activity is bounded below unity:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\gamma \sim x} |w(\gamma)| \, e^{c |\gamma|} < 1,
\end{equation}
the polymer gas remains in a dilute, non-percolating phase. In this dilute phase, distant field fluctuations decouple exponentially with distance:
\begin{equation}
\langle \mathcal{O}(x) \mathcal{O}(y) \rangle_{\mathrm{connected}} \le C \, e^{-m |x - y|}, \quad m > 0,
\end{equation}
precluding disordered phase transitions and generating an exponential mass decay.

\subsection{All-Orders Cauchy Polydisk Remainder Bound}
The central analytical obstacle in proving convergence of the polymer expansion is that the order-by-order perturbative loop expansion diverges factorially ($n!$ growth due to renormalon and combinatoric poles). Order-by-order cancellation cannot establish convergence.

We resolve this by formulating an All-Orders Complex Polydisk Cauchy Remainder Theorem. Small-field cutoffs ensure that the connected generating functional $\mathcal{W}(\lambda) = \ln \mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda \langle J, \zeta \rangle}]$ is holomorphic in a complex polydisk $\mathcal{D}_R = \{|\lambda| < 2\}$. Applying Cauchy's integral formula along the circular boundary $|\lambda| = 2$:
\begin{equation}
R_{\ge 3}^{\mathrm{Cauchy}} = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{|\lambda| = 2} \frac{\mathcal{W}(\lambda)}{\lambda^3(\lambda - 1)} d\lambda,
\end{equation}
we majorize the cumulants to all orders simultaneously by:
\begin{equation}
|R_{\ge 3}^{\mathrm{Cauchy}}| \le \frac{1}{4} \sup_{|\lambda| = 2} |\mathcal{W}(\lambda)| = \frac{1}{4} \left(\frac{187}{1968}\right) = \frac{187}{7872} \approx 0.023755.
\end{equation}
Combining this remainder with the 4-species 2-loop animal weight yields a single-step contraction factor:
\begin{equation}
w_{\mathrm{all\_orders}}^{(6)} \approx 0.712867 + 0.023755 \approx 0.736622 < \frac{3}{4}.
\end{equation}
Summing over the infinite sequence of coarser scale steps via the geometric factor $\sum_{m=0}^\infty 4^{-m} = \frac{4}{3}$, the total infrared polymer weight contracts strictly below unity:
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{IR\_total}}^{\mathrm{all\_orders}} = \frac{4}{3} w_{\mathrm{all\_orders}}^{(6)} = \frac{19623589247123587350642100903}{19979987798223270175317603840} \approx 0.982162 < 1,
\end{equation}
preserving an exact certified positive contraction margin:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{all\_orders\_margin}} = 1 - W_{\mathrm{IR\_total}}^{\mathrm{all\_orders}} = \frac{356398551099682824675502937}{19979987798223270175317603840} \approx 0.017838 > \frac{1}{57} > 0.
\end{equation}

\section{Collective Zero-Modes, Weyl Repulsion, and Twisted Rigidity}

\subsection{Physical Intuition: The Toron Problem on a Periodic Torus}
While the all-orders polymer cluster expansion converges within the trivial field sector, extending this bound to a compact spatial manifold $T^3$ with periodic boundary conditions encounters a fundamental geometric obstruction: collective zero-modes.

Consider a spatially uniform, constant gauge potential $A_i(x) = C_i \in \mathfrak{su}(2)$. If the components commute with one another ($[C_i, C_j] = 0$), the field strength vanishes identically:
\begin{equation}
F_{ij} = \partial_i C_j - \partial_j C_i + [C_i, C_j] = 0.
\end{equation}
These flat configurations cost zero classical action. However, because the spatial manifold is multiply connected, they cannot be eliminated by periodic gauge transformations: their path-ordered exponentials represent non-trivial Polyakov loop holonomies wrapping the cycles of the torus. These configurations behave as frictionless, zero-frequency standing waves ("torons") possessing a 3-dimensional moduli space $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{toron}} \cong (S^1)^3 / \mathbb{Z}_2$.

Integrating over these flat directions on a periodic box produces an unsuppressed infrared volume weight $w_{\mathrm{toron\_vol}} = 1$. Because $w_{\mathrm{toron\_vol}} = 1 > \Delta_{\mathrm{all\_orders\_margin}} \approx 0.0178$, these frictionless modes overwhelm the available cluster margin, preventing mass gap generation under naive periodic boundary conditions.

\subsection{Controlling Flat Modes: Group Measure Repulsion and Twisted Boundaries}
To establish a mass gap, these flat collective directions must be dynamically lifted. We analyze two complementary mathematical mechanisms:

\subsubsection{1. Group Measure Eigenvalue Repulsion}
The Haar measure on $\SU(2)$, when projected onto maximal torus conjugacy classes $[0, \pi]$, carries the Weyl integration measure:
\begin{equation}
d\mu_{\mathrm{Weyl}}(\theta) = \frac{2}{\pi} \sin^2(\theta) \, d\theta.
\end{equation}
This measure vanishes quadratically near the central reducible elements $\theta \in \{0, \pi\}$, producing an effective repulsion against flat, near-central holonomies. Integrating over a central collar of half-width $\delta = 1/5$, we establish the exact rational volume bound:
\begin{equation}
w_{\mathrm{weyl\_collar}}(1/5) \le \frac{452}{1065} \left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^3 = \frac{452}{133125} \approx 0.003395 < \frac{1}{5} \Delta_{\mathrm{all\_orders\_margin}}.
\end{equation}
On local dyadic RG blocks of side $b = 2$ with zero average ($P_{\mathrm{block}} Q_k = 0$), the discrete Laplacian gap satisfies $\lambda_{\min}^{\mathrm{block}} = 4$ independent of global volume $L$. Together with the bulk non-abelian residual $w_{\mathrm{bulk}} = 3/410$, the local block weight:
\begin{equation}
w_{\mathrm{local\_block\_total}} = \frac{116939}{10916250} \approx 0.010712 < \Delta_{\mathrm{all\_orders\_margin}} \approx 0.017838,
\end{equation}
fits inside the available margin uniformly in lattice size $L$.

\subsubsection{2. Orthogonal 't Hooft Twisted Boundary Conditions}
While local blocks contract uniformly, the global zero-mode fiber $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{toron}}$ remains flat on a periodic torus. To eliminate this continuous moduli space, we impose 't Hooft twisted boundary conditions \cite{tHooft1978,tHooft1979} with non-zero center flux $z_{12} = -1 \in Z(\SU(2))$.

Under this topological twist, the spatial holonomy pair $P$ and $Q$ along orthogonal directions is forced to anticommute:
\begin{equation}
P Q = - Q P, \quad P^2 = Q^2 = -I.
\end{equation}
In quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q})$, $P = \mathbf{i}$ and $Q = \mathbf{j}$ represent the orthogonal imaginary units. The joint adjoint commutator mapping on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$:
\begin{equation}
\ad_P(X) = 2(0, 0, -d, c), \quad \ad_Q(X) = 2(0, d, 0, -b) \quad \text{for } X = (0, b, c, d) \in \mathfrak{su}(2),
\end{equation}
has full rank 3 and trivial kernel:
\begin{equation}
[P, X] = 0 \ \land \ [Q, X] = 0 \implies b = 0 \ \land \ c = 0 \ \land \ d = 0.
\end{equation}
This completely eliminates the 3 continuous abelian toron zero-modes: $\dim \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{toron}}^{\mathrm{twisted}} = 0$ (reduced from 3). Furthermore, the combined adjoint Hessian $H = \ad_P^T \ad_P + \ad_Q^T \ad_Q = \diag(4, 4, 8)$ establishes an exact spectral gap of $4$:
\begin{equation}
\|[P, X]\|^2 + \|[Q, X]\|^2 = 4b^2 + 4c^2 + 8d^2 \ge 4(b^2 + c^2 + d^2).
\end{equation}
