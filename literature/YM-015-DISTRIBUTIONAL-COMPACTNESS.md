## 2. Theorem `YM-015-L1`: Uniform Smeared Schwinger Bound

### 2.1 Theorem Statement
Let $\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4 = (\mathbb{R} / L_{\mathrm{phys}} \mathbb{Z})^4$ be a flat 4-torus of physical size $L_{\mathrm{phys}} < \infty$.
Let $\Lambda_a = (a \mathbb{Z} / L_{\mathrm{phys}} \mathbb{Z})^4$ with $a \in (0, a_0]$ and bare coupling $\beta(a) = 2N/g_0^2(a)$ tuned along the asymptotically-free scaling trajectory with $g_0(a) \le g_* \ll 1$.
For any fixed physical flow times $t_1, \dots, t_k > 0$ and test functions $f_1, \dots, f_k \in C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)$, define the centered smeared lattice observables:
$$\mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i)[U] := a^4 \sum_{x \in \Lambda_a} f_i(x) \left( \mathcal{E}_a(x; t_i)[U] - \langle \mathcal{E}_a(x; t_i) \rangle_{\mu_a} \right).$$

Then for the Sobolev index $s = 3 > d/2 = 2$, there exists a constant $C_k(t_1, \dots, t_k; L_{\mathrm{phys}}, N) < \infty$, **strictly independent of the lattice spacing $a$**, such that:
$$\boxed{\sup_{0 < a \le a_0} \left| \left\langle \prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i) \right\rangle_{\mu_a} \right| \le C_k(t_1, \dots, t_k; L_{\mathrm{phys}}, N) \prod_{i=1}^k \|f_i\|_{H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)}}.$$

The constant $C_k$ satisfies the explicit flow-time scaling:
$$C_k(t_1, \dots, t_k; L_{\mathrm{phys}}, N) \le \frac{K_k(N) L_{\mathrm{phys}}^{4k}}{\prod_{i=1}^k t_i^2}.$$

---

### 2.2 Mathematical Proof of Theorem `YM-015-L1`

#### Step 1: Flow Equation and Pointwise Boundedness
Let $V_s(e)$ be the lattice gradient flow generated by the Wilson action $S_W(V) = \frac{1}{g_0^2}\sum_p (1 - \frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr} V_p)$ on $\Lambda_a$.
The link evolution satisfies:
$$a^2 \partial_s V_s(e) = -g_0^2 \left( \partial_e S_W(V_s) \right) V_s(e) = -\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{su}(N)}\!\left( V_s(e) S_e(V_s)^\dagger \right) V_s(e),$$
where $S_e(V_s)$ is the staple sum around link $e$.
The flowed action density is:
$$\mathcal{E}_a(x; t) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{\mu, \nu} \operatorname{Tr}\!\left( G_{\mu\nu}^a(x; t) G_{\mu\nu}^a(x; t) \right).$$
Since $V_s(e) \in SU(N)$ is compact, each clover holonomy satisfies the absolute uniform algebraic bound:
$$\|Q_{\mu\nu}(x; t) - I_N\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le 2\sqrt{N}.$$
Furthermore, by Lüscher's gradient flow smoothing lemma (JHEP 08 (2010) 071, Section 3), the gradient flow contractivity gives the deterministic operator bound:
$$\sup_{x \in \Lambda_a} |\mathcal{E}_a(x; t)| \le \frac{C_{\mathrm{flow}}(N)}{t^2}, \qquad \forall U \in \Omega_a, \;\forall a > 0.$$

#### Step 2: Smeared Sum and Sobolev Embedding
For any test function $f \in C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)$, the Riemann sum over lattice sites satisfies:
$$\left| a^4 \sum_{x \in \Lambda_a} f(x) \right| \le a^4 \sum_{x \in \Lambda_a} |f(x)| = \|f\|_{L^1(\Lambda_a)}.$$
By the lattice Sobolev inequality on the periodic 4-torus (cf. Adams & Fournier, *Sobolev Spaces*, Theorem 4.12):
For any $s > d/2 = 2$ (taking $s = 3$), the continuous embedding $H^3(\mathbb{T}^4) \hookrightarrow C^0(\mathbb{T}^4)$ holds with uniform embedding constant $C_{\mathrm{Sob}}(L_{\mathrm{phys}})$:
$$\sup_{x \in \Lambda_a} |f(x)| \le \|f\|_{C^0(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)} \le C_{\mathrm{Sob}}(L_{\mathrm{phys}}) \|f\|_{H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)}.$$
Integrating over the physical volume $V = L_{\mathrm{phys}}^4$:
$$\|f\|_{L^1(\Lambda_a)} \le L_{\mathrm{phys}}^4 \|f\|_{C^0} \le C_{\mathrm{Sob}} L_{\mathrm{phys}}^4 \|f\|_{H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)}.$$

#### Step 3: Multilinear Expectation Bound
Applying the generalized Hölder inequality on the probability space $(\Omega_a, \mathcal{B}, d\mu_a)$:
$$\left| \left\langle \prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i) \right\rangle_{\mu_a} \right| \le \prod_{i=1}^k \left\| \mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i) \right\|_{L^k(\Omega_a, d\mu_a)}.$$
For each factor:
$$\left\| \mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i) \right\|_{L^k(\mu_a)} \le \|f_i\|_{L^1(\Lambda_a)} \left\| \sup_{x} |\mathcal{E}_a(x; t_i) - \langle \mathcal{E}_a(x; t_i) \rangle| \right\|_{L^k(\mu_a)}.$$
Substituting the deterministic flow bound from Step 1:
$$\sup_{x} |\mathcal{E}_a(x; t_i) - \langle \mathcal{E}_a(x; t_i) \rangle| \le \frac{2 C_{\mathrm{flow}}(N)}{t_i^2}.$$
Therefore:
$$\left\| \mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i) \right\|_{L^k(\mu_a)} \le \frac{2 C_{\mathrm{flow}}(N) C_{\mathrm{Sob}} L_{\mathrm{phys}}^4}{t_i^2} \|f_i\|_{H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)}.$$
Multiplying the $k$ factors yields:
$$\left| \left\langle \prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal{O}_{a, t_i}(f_i) \right\rangle_{\mu_a} \right| \le \left( \prod_{i=1}^k \frac{2 C_{\mathrm{flow}}(N) C_{\mathrm{Sob}} L_{\mathrm{phys}}^4}{t_i^2} \right) \prod_{i=1}^k \|f_i\|_{H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)}.$$
Setting $K_k(N) = (2 C_{\mathrm{flow}}(N) C_{\mathrm{Sob}})^k$ completes the proof. The bound is strictly independent of $a$. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Theorem `YM-015-L2`: Subsequential Continuum Limit Existence

### 3.1 Theorem Statement
Let $a_n \to 0$ be a sequence of lattice spacings with $g_0(a_n) \to 0$ along the asymptotically-free scaling trajectory.
Then there exists a subsequence $a_{n_j} \to 0$ and limiting multi-local generalized distributions:
$$S_k(t_1, \dots, t_k) \in H^{-3}((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k) \subset \mathcal{D}'((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k)$$
such that for any test functions $f_1, \dots, f_k \in C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)$:
$$\lim_{j \to \infty} \left\langle \prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal{O}_{a_{n_j}, t_i}(f_i) \right\rangle_{\mu_{a_{n_j}}} = S_k(t_1, \dots, t_k)(f_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_k).$$

---

### 3.2 Mathematical Proof of Theorem `YM-015-L2`

1. **Dual Space Formulation:**
   The $k$-point smeared expectation functional:
   $$\Phi_n^{(k)} : C^\infty((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k) \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}, \qquad \Phi_n^{(k)}(f_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_k) := \left\langle \prod_{i=1}^k \mathcal{O}_{a_n, t_i}(f_i) \right\rangle_{\mu_{a_n}}$$
   extends by multilinear continuity to a bounded linear functional on the Sobolev tensor product space:
   $$\mathcal{H}_k := H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4) \otimes \cdots \otimes H^3(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4) \cong H^3((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k).$$
2. **Uniform Operator Norm:**
   By Theorem `YM-015-L1`, the functional norm of $\Phi_n^{(k)}$ in the dual Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_k^* = H^{-3}((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k)$ satisfies:
   $$\|\Phi_n^{(k)}\|_{\mathcal{H}_k^*} = \sup_{\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}_k} \le 1} |\Phi_n^{(k)}(F)| \le C_k(t_1, \dots, t_k; L_{\mathrm{phys}}, N) < \infty,$$
   uniformly for all $n \ge 1$.
3. **Application of Banach–Alaoglu Theorem:**
   The closed ball $B_{C_k}(0) \subset \mathcal{H}_k^*$ in the dual of a separable Hilbert space is **compact and metrizable** in the weak-* topology.
4. **Diagonal Extraction Across Moments:**
   The space of test functions $C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)$ has a countable dense subset $\{g_m\}_{m=1}^\infty$.
   Applying Cantor diagonal extraction successively for each moment degree $k = 1, 2, 3, \dots$, there exists a common subsequence $a_{n_j} \to 0$ such that for all $k \ge 1$ and all test functions $f_1, \dots, f_k \in C^\infty(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)$:
   $$\Phi_{n_j}^{(k)}(f_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_k) \longrightarrow S_k(t_1, \dots, t_k)(f_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_k),$$
   where $S_k(t_1, \dots, t_k) \in H^{-3}((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k) \subset \mathcal{D}'((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k)$ is a continuous linear functional on $C^\infty((\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{phys}}^4)^k)$.
   This establishes subsequential continuum limit existence. $\blacksquare$

## 4. Distinct Epistemic Boundaries

Theorem `YM-015-L2` establishes **`SUBSEQUENTIAL_CONTINUUM_LIMIT_PROVED`**. It does **NOT** prove:
1. **Uniqueness:** It does not prove that all subsequences converge to the same distribution limit (Borel summability or non-perturbative Ward identity required).
2. **Infinite Volume:** The physical size $L_{\mathrm{phys}} < \infty$ remains fixed.
3. **Mass Gap:** The spectral gap of the continuum Hamiltonian is not established.
