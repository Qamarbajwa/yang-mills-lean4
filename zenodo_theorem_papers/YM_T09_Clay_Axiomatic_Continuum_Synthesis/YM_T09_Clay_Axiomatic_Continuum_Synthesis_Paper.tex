\section{The Three Classical Roadblocks and Their Fiber Bundle Resolutions}

For five decades, constructive quantum field theory attempted an additive measure construction on distribution spaces $\mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^4)$. Every attempt stalled against three roadblocks:

\subsection{Roadblock 1: The Gribov Ambiguity}
In 1978, V. N. Gribov proved that in non-abelian gauge theories, no continuous, global gauge-fixing condition exists. Every gauge slice intersects the gauge orbit multiple times (Gribov copies).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Resolution:} All Gribov copies reside along the flat abelian directions of the gauge group. Under our Three-Split premise, we treat the configuration space as a fiber bundle:
    $$\mathcal{M} = \frac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{G}} \cong \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{non-abelian}} \times \left( \frac{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{abelian}}}{\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{abelian}}} \right)$$

Any discrete lattice breaks continuous rotation down to the discrete hypercubic group $H_4$. Proving that rotational invariance is restored in the limit $a \to 0$ has been notoriously difficult.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Resolution:} We apply the **Lüscher Yang-Mills gradient flow** equation:
    $$\partial_t B_\mu = D_\nu G_{\nu\mu}, \quad B_\mu(0, x) = A_\mu(x)$$

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Roadblock 3: Large-Field Non-Perturbative Entropy and the $R \to \infty$ Limit}

\begin{itemize}
