# YM-015F-JET-001 — source derivatives and the insertion norm

Evidence: PROVED_WITHIN_STATED_HYPOTHESES (elementary conditional reduction), pending independent review. This is not YM-015F-L1.

Fix k, paired observables (t_i,f_i), SU(N), and finite physical volume. Set p(f)=||f||_{H^3}. If p(f_i)=0 the observable vanishes. Otherwise write X_i=O_{a,t_i}(f_i)/p(f_i). On each fixed finite lattice X_i is bounded, although its bound can diverge as a decreases. Define the normalized source partition function

    F_a(z) = E_mu_a exp(sum_i z_i X_i),    F_a(0)=1.

It is entire in the k complex sources for each fixed a. Differentiation under the finite-lattice integral yields

    partial_1 ... partial_k F_a(0) = E_mu_a product_i X_i.

Conditional lemma J1. If there exist r_i>0 and M_k<infinity, independent of a and of the normalized test functions, such that |F_a(z)|<=M_k on the closed polydisc |z_i|<=r_i, the iterated Cauchy integral formula gives

    |E product_i O_i| <= M_k product_i r_i^{-1} product_i p(f_i).

Proof: integrate F_a(z) product_i dz_i/z_i^2 over the product of circles. Each circle contributes a factor r_i^{-1}; multiply back the p(f_i). This is nonperturbative if its hypotheses hold for the actual Gibbs measures. No partition-function or flow estimate proves these hypotheses here.

FALSIFY: J1 is sufficient, not necessary for finite moments of all orders. A lognormal random variable has all finite moments but E exp(rX)=infinity for every r>0. Requiring a uniform analytic source radius can overstrengthen YM-015F.

Therefore select the finite source-jet alternative J2. Work in the commutative algebra generated by z_1,...,z_k with z_i^2=0. In this algebra

    exp(sum_i z_i X_i) = product_i (1+z_i X_i).

Its z_S coefficient is E product_{i in S} X_i. With weights r_i>0 define

    ||P||_r = sum_{S subset {1,...,k}} |P_S| product_{i in S} r_i.

This norm is submultiplicative: expand each coefficient as the sum over disjoint A,B with A union B=S, apply the triangle inequality, and bound the resulting sum by the unrestricted product of coefficient sums. The full coefficient is at most ||P||_r / product_i r_i. This is an exact algebraic identity, not an infinite exponential-moment requirement. Repeated physical observables can use distinct nilpotent source labels.
